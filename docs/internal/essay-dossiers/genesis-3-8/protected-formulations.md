# Genesis 3:8 Protected Formulations

Codex was correct that merely referencing PF-3.8-01–19 was insufficient. The complete list follows.

## Tier 1 — Wording substantially protected

These formulations should remain substantially as written. Minor punctuation or mechanical corrections are acceptable, but implementation should not rewrite their language or replace them with technical equivalents.

### PF-3.8-01 — Behavior before motive

> **“At this point in the story, we know what the humans do. We do not yet know why they do it.”**

**Reason protected:** Plain-language expression of the behavior/motive boundary and the Genesis 3:8 evidence horizon.

### PF-3.8-02 — Human withdrawal

> **“The humans withdraw from God. But Genesis has not yet shown God withdrawing from them.”**

**Reason protected:** Accessible expression of the central relational asymmetry without assigning motive to God.

### PF-3.8-03 — The same Person

> **“The God connected to the boundary is also the God connected to the garden. The God who warned is also the God who provided.”**

**Reason protected:** Makes Subject-Identity Continuity understandable without methodological terminology.

### PF-3.8-04 — Expectation is not verification

> **“The response may tell you what the first person expects. It does not prove that the expectation is correct.”**

**Reason protected:** Critical explanatory bridge into Perceived Character ≠ Demonstrated Character.

### PF-3.8-05 — Hiding cannot define God

> **“Their hiding cannot, by itself, tell us what God is like.”**

**Reason protected:** Central Character-of-God boundary in accessible language.

### PF-3.8-06 — Named distinction

> **“Perceived Character is not the same as Demonstrated Character.”**

**Reason protected:** Names the methodological discovery after the essay has already explained it.

**Implementation safeguard:** Do not move this formulation ahead of its ordinary-language explanation.

### PF-3.8-07 — God must supply the evidence about God

> **“God's behavior must be allowed to speak for God.”**

**Reason protected:** Accessible expression of the evidentiary principle underlying the essay.

### PF-3.8-08 — Adjectives require evidence

> **“Let God's behavior earn the adjective.”**

**Reason protected:** Governing rhetorical restraint inherited directly from the authoritative investigation. The dossier itself identifies this as the stronger posture after refusing premature labels such as gentleness, love, restraint, anger, hostility, and mercy. authoritative-investigation-dos…

### PF-3.8-09 — Neither defense nor accusation

> **“We do not need to defend God before seeing what he does. We also do not need to accuse him before seeing what he does.”**

**Reason protected:** Explains PF-3.8-08 and prevents the memorable formulation from becoming compressed meaning.

### PF-3.8-10 — Reader expectation

> **“What do you expect God to do?”**

and

> **“What made you expect that?”**

**Reason protected:** Central public expression of the Relational/Reader Lens. The second question turns the lens toward the reader's own expectation rather than merely asking for a prediction.

This relationship was already present in the original handoff: the reader was to be asked what they expect God to do and then what in Genesis produced that expectation. Pasted text(1)

### PF-3.8-11 — Salience is not evidence

> **“Those experiences can affect what we notice and what we expect. They are worth paying attention to. But they are not automatically evidence about God.”**

**Reason protected:** Accessible enactment of the project's established salience/evidence distinction.

### PF-3.8-12 — Return the lens to God

> **“We have seen what the humans do. Now we need to see what God does.”**

**Reason protected:** States the precise epistemic position at the end of Genesis 3:8 without stealing Genesis 3:9.

### PF-3.8-13 — Closing formulation

> **“Keep watching him.”**

**Reason protected:** Final line of the essay.

In this essay it functions as the consequence of the investigation, not as a promotional slogan. The reader has reached a genuine evidentiary boundary: human response is visible; God's subsequent response is not.

# Tier 2 — Meaning and rhetorical function protected

These passages may receive minor implementation editing if technically necessary, but their **meaning, location, and argumentative function** must survive.

### PF-3.8-14 — Provision and concealment

Preserve the movement:

**garden/trees as prior provision → same trees as location of concealment**

Do not restore:

- “tool”;
- “weapon”;
- deliberate use of God's gifts against God;
- other instrumental psychology.

RR-3.8-02 specifically narrowed the earlier formulation for this reason. authoritative-investigation-dos…

### PF-3.8-15 — Agency after misuse

Preserve the explanation that crossing the boundary did not erase the humans' ability to make consequential choices.

Do not restore the rejected characterization:

> “God's approach is non-overwhelming.”

The authorized claim concerns continuing human agency regardless of unresolved sensory intensity.

### PF-3.8-16 — Translation uncertainty without technical takeover

Preserve the essay's brief acknowledgment that:

- translations differ;
- the ancient wording permits unresolved questions;
- scholarly alternatives exist;
- the central encounter nevertheless remains sufficiently clear.

Do not turn this passage into a Hebrew word study or storm-theophany excursus.

The underlying investigation can carry that technical detail. The public essay does not need to.

### PF-3.8-17 — Adjective restraint

Preserve the essay's consideration of character descriptions such as:

- angry;
- gentle;
- merciful;
- loving;
- hostile;
- forgiving.

Their rhetorical function is to show that the reader may want to characterize God **before Genesis 3:8 has supplied enough evidence to choose the controlling adjective**.

Do not silently select one during editing.

### PF-3.8-18 — Sources of reader expectation

Preserve the reader-facing invitation to consider possible sources of expectation, including:

- church teaching;
- childhood;
- other Scripture;
- experiences with parents, teachers, pastors, or other authority figures;
- personality;
- personal experience.

Do not automatically rename these influences **biases**.

They may generate salience, expectations, questions, or candidate insights. Their presence does not by itself establish either error or truth.

### PF-3.8-19 — Stop before Genesis 3:9

Preserve the explicit instruction to stop before reading the next verse.

This is not merely a dramatic device.

It gives the reader an opportunity to discover:

1. what they expect God to do;
2. what produced that expectation;
3. which part of that expectation comes from Genesis 1:1–3:8;
4. which part may have come from somewhere else.

Only after that examination should God's next behavior arrive as new evidence.

The original handoff explicitly required the essay to end while this question remained alive. Pasted text(1)
