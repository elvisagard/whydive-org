Yes. I would build these as the **public claim-audit tables**, not simply reproduce the internal adjudication. The public tables should expose the claims that materially govern each essay, show their confidence, explain why we retained them, and make serious external dialogue inspectable.

I would also keep **open questions in the tables** where the essay deliberately refuses to resolve something. That makes the restraint auditable rather than invisible.

That conclusion is strong without claiming that holy time is the ultimate purpose of creation.

### Governing public conclusion

The synthesis essay is not arguing:

> Genesis answered all the important questions.

It is arguing something much more bounded:

> **Genesis leaves enormous territories unexplained while making a relatively small set of highly consequential claims unusually clear. Those claims repeatedly direct the reader toward the Creator, giving inquiry provisional anchors without requiring the unanswered questions to disappear.**

## One implementation decision I recommend

For the public site, I would **not place these full tables directly beneath the essays**. They are too dense for the reading experience we deliberately designed.

Instead, each essay should have a compact **Claim Status** table containing perhaps:

**Claim | Status | External dialogue | Rationale**

with **Rationale** linking to a dedicated claim-audit route where the complete record lives.

That preserves the essay as an essay while making every consequential claim inspectable.

And importantly, the tables above now give us the governed source from which those public compact tables and detailed rationale pages can be generated.
