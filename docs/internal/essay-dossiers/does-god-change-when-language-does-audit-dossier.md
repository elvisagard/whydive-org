## Final Argument Records

### AR-01 --- The Person Remains Recognizable Across the Tested Witnesses

**Status:** High-confidence finding / translation-resilient within
tested corpus.

Across Genesis 1:1--5 witnesses examined, substantial
lexical/descriptive variation occurred while central narrated behaviors
remained comparatively stable: God creates, remains involved, speaks,
expresses volition, sees, positively appraises light, differentiates,
names, and continues acting.

Complications included *tohu wabohu*, *ruach Elohim*, *tov*, temporal
formulas, narrative economy, fulfillment placement, and second-day
appraisal. The evidence does not authorize "God's character is
language-independent," identical translation content, or identical
reader perception.

**RR:** RR-01.\
**Protected formulation:** "The vocabulary moved more than the Person
did."

### AR-02 --- Significant Character Evidence Is Available to Non-Specialist Readers

**Status:** Strong methodological inference / translation-resilient
evidence base.

Translated texts directly preserve major narrated actions. A reader may
therefore begin meaningful character inquiry without prior
source-language specialization. The study did not establish that every
reader will notice or interpret the evidence similarly.

**Protected formulation:** "You do not need to become a scholar before
you are allowed to start looking."

### AR-03 --- Textual Availability Is Not Reader Perception

**Status:** High-confidence methodological boundary / negative finding.

The experiment compared texts and translations; it did not study
independent native-language readers. Comparable textual evidence is
available; comparable reader perception has not been demonstrated.

**OQ:** OQ-01.

### AR-04 --- Expressed Divine Volition Corresponds With Resulting Reality

**Status:** High-confidence finding / translation-resilient.

Genesis 1:3 gives the reader access to divine volition concerning light
before corresponding reality. Korean translation discussion made
volition newly salient; Hebrew examination supported retaining
"volition/intention" without making "command" wrong.

**Reader effect:** the reader can compare what God expressed with what
occurred.\
**Purpose boundary:** the investigation does not establish that God
disclosed intention specifically to build trust.

### AR-05 --- Positive Appraisal Is Stable; Exact Lexical Content Is Not

**Status:** High-confidence behavioral finding / translation-resilient;
lexical resolution translation-sensitive.

God sees light and communicates positive appraisal. The investigation
did not reduce *tov* exhaustively to moral goodness, functionality,
beauty, usefulness, or perfection.

**Local-confidence rule:** uncertainty about lexical breadth does not
erase the narrated act of appraisal.

### AR-06 --- Differentiation, Naming, and Progressive Activity Remain Stable

**Status:** High-confidence behavioral findings / strong structural
inference / translation-resilient.

God differentiates light/darkness, names Day/Night, and continues
creative activity after demonstrating effective agency.

**Strong inference:** demonstrated efficacy coexists with progressive
activity.\
**Boundary:** why God chooses progression remains open.

### AR-07 --- Local Translation Differences Require Local Restraint

**Status:** High-confidence translation-sensitive finding.

*Tohu wabohu*: "unfinished" is a strong synthesis, not an exhaustive
lexical equivalent.

*Ruach Elohim*: Spirit/wind possibilities require restraint; no
developed creative mechanism is established.

Narrative economy: economical narration does not by itself establish
divine humility, modesty, or absence of boasting.

> **Local uncertainty stays local.**

### AR-08 --- The Day-Two Appraisal Is a Genuine Textual Complication

**Status:** High-confidence negative finding /
textual-tradition-sensitive.

The MT absence of positive appraisal cannot bear substantial unqualified
character theology because the LXX tradition can contain the appraisal.
The MT silence remains observable but cannot be universalized.

**RR:** RR-04.

### AR-09 --- Another Language Can Illuminate Without Becoming Authoritative

**Status:** Strong methodological finding.

The Korean Genesis 1:3 case demonstrated the process:

**salience → candidate question → return to source evidence → adjudicate
→ retain/revise/reject.**

Translation may preserve, obscure, interpret, or illuminate.
Illumination does not mean the target translation outranks the source.

### AR-10 --- Reader Location Can Affect Salience Without Authorizing Meaning

**Status:** Strong inference / externally supported reader-perception
principle.

Language, culture, prior theology, education, profession, personality,
and life experience can affect salience. The Genesis experiment did not
experimentally vary actual readers across all these dimensions.

> **Salience generates questions and candidate discoveries. Evidence
> adjudicates them.**

### AR-11 --- "Show Me Where You Saw That" Is a Disciplined Invitation

**Status:** Methodological application / praxis recommendation.

Asking another reader to locate an observation in shared textual
evidence welcomes difference without treating every interpretation as
equally authorized.

The investigation did not demonstrate greater accuracy, consensus,
humility, or completeness from collaborative reading.

**Protected formulation:** "Show me where you saw that."

## Open Questions

**OQ-01:** Would independent readers using different languages
spontaneously perceive substantially similar character patterns?

**OQ-02:** Which dimensions of culture and experience materially affect
character salience?

**OQ-03:** Can disciplined cross-cultural group reading enlarge
perception without increasing interpretive distortion?

**OQ-04:** Does linguistic plurality have any deliberate theological
relationship to multiple perceptual angles on revelation?

## Binding Boundaries

1.  Effect is not purpose.
2.  Illumination is not authorization.
3.  Textual availability is not reader performance.
4.  Cultural salience is not cultural truth.
5.  Cross-cultural reading is an invitation, not a validated outcome.
6.  Local uncertainty stays local.
7.  Mystery does not automatically reduce confidence in what is
    disclosed.
8.  Silence is not negation.

## Published-Prose Reconciliation

### Required

If the essay still says **"God evaluates it positively,"** change to
**"God appraises it positively."**

### Recommended

If the essay still says **"It was teaching me where to become less
certain,"** revise toward:

> **"It was teaching me not simply to become less certain, but to become
> more precise about where my uncertainty belonged."**

No other substantive prose changes are presently required.

## Protected Formulations

-   The vocabulary moved more than the Person did.
-   Notice the silence, but follow the speech.
-   Never protect a discovery from another language.
-   Another language had not given us a new doctrine.
-   Sometimes translation preserves. Sometimes it obscures. Sometimes it
    interprets. And sometimes it illuminates.
-   Sometimes uncertainty at the microscope level does not destroy
    confidence at the wide-angle level.
-   Salience generates questions and candidate discoveries. Evidence
    adjudicates them.
-   Show me where you saw that.
-   Confidence should grow in proportion to what a discovery survives.
-   You do not need to become a scholar before you are allowed to start
    looking.
-   Maybe together we will see a little more.
