# Genesis 2:4–25 — Final Argument Records

## CA-G2-01 — Grounded Lordship

**Claim:** The Creator's lordship is already grounded before Genesis 2 begins sustained human-divine interaction.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Scope:** Genesis 1:1–2:4 as accumulated revealed history entering Genesis 2:4–25.

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 1 presents God as the sole originating creator and determining authority within the disclosed created order. Genesis 2:4 then identifies **YHWH Elohim** as the maker of earth and heavens.

**Reasoning:** Creatorship is the elemental foundation. God does not purchase, inherit, conquer, or receive creation from a prior owner. Ownership follows naturally from creatorship; lordship over what he created therefore requires no external authorization.

**External support:** Even scholarship that treats Genesis 1 and Genesis 2–3 as compositionally distinct recognizes that the combined text deliberately identifies YHWH Elohim of Genesis 2–3 with the Elohim of Genesis 1. One source-critical treatment argues that *Elohim* was attached to YHWH precisely to highlight the shared identity across the two accounts. ([TheTorah.com][1])

**External challenge:** Source criticism identifies substantial vocabulary, style, sequence, and compositional differences between Genesis 1 and 2.

**Adjudication:** Literary-source distinction does not constitute counterevidence to the canonical relationship actually presented to the reader. The claim concerns the accumulated disclosure, not common literary authorship.

**Claim boundary:** Genesis has not yet presented an abstract theory of divine property rights.

**Rejected overclaim:** “Genesis 2 proves God's lordship because the word LORD appears.” The argument is cumulative, not lexical.

**Methodology controls:** Wide-Angle Lens; Revelatory Anchor; Anchored Inquiry; External Alternative ≠ Counterevidence.

## CA-G2-02 — Beneficent Lordship

**Claim:** The LORD repeatedly exercises his authority beneficently toward the human.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Scope:** Genesis 2:4–25 against Genesis 1's accumulated portrait.

**Textual evidence:** God forms and gives life; establishes a place; causes food-bearing and visually desirable trees to grow; provides water; grants responsibility; permits broadly; warns of grave consequence; notices aloneness; and establishes corresponding human relationship.

**Reasoning:** These actions repeatedly operate toward conditions favorable to human life, function, provision, protection, and relationship. Beneficence is therefore behavioral evidence rather than an inferred emotional state.

**External support:** Scholarship on Genesis 2:7 recognizes that the human becomes living through God's life-giving action; importantly, the “living being” terminology does not by itself distinguish humans from all other living creatures. ([OUP Academic][2]) Scholarship on the garden likewise recognizes that God plants, causes desirable/food-bearing trees to grow, and places the human there. ([Bible Gateway][3])

**External challenge:** Individual actions could be assigned other theological interpretations, and scholarship does not universally organize Genesis 2 under “beneficence.”

**Adjudication:** The claim does not depend upon scholarship adopting our synthesis. It arises cumulatively from multiple independent textual relationships. No serious alternative examined explains those actions as harmful to the human at this stage.

**Claim boundary:** Beneficence does **not yet equal** the stronger proposition “God is love.”

**Rejected overclaim:** “Every action of God in Genesis 2 has already been proven loving.”

**Methodology controls:** Cumulative Evidence; Local Confidence; Character/Nature/Reality distinction; Claim-Distance Control.

## CA-G2-03 — Proactive, Protective, and Attentive Care

**Claim:** God's beneficence includes proactive provision, protective warning, and attentive care.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** Provision precedes narrated request. The death warning precedes the prohibited action. God identifies the human's aloneness as “not good” before any human complaint and immediately announces action concerning it.

**Reasoning:** These are three distinguishable behavioral expressions of beneficence:

**Proactive:** benefit precedes request.
**Protective:** information relevant to avoiding grave consequence precedes the action.
**Attentive:** God notices and acts concerning a negatively appraised creaturely condition.

**External support:** The Hebrew of Genesis 2:18 explicitly predicates *lo tov*—“not good”—of the human's being alone and follows it with God's first-person intention, “I will make.” ([Fellowshipbook][4]) The emphatic death construction in 2:17 strengthens the seriousness of the advance warning. ([Tanakh][5])

**External challenge:** “Care” could be criticized as importing an emotional state into behavioral description.

**Adjudication:** The claim is intentionally behavioral. Warning someone beforehand of a grave consequence gives information useful for avoiding that consequence; recognizing and acting concerning a “not good” condition functions beneficially whether or not the text discloses God's emotional psychology.

**Claim boundary:** No exhaustive claim about divine emotion is made.

**Methodology controls:** Effect ≠ Purpose; Behavioral Evidence; Local Confidence.

## CA-G2-04 — Dignity and Dependence

**Claim:** Human dignity and creaturely dependence are simultaneously maintained.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Accumulated evidence:** Genesis 1 gives humanity image, likeness, blessing, and dominion. Genesis 2:7 gives dust from the ground, divine formation, divine breath, and received life.

**Reasoning:** Genesis does not allow either disclosure to cancel the other. The image-bearing creature is earth-derived and receives life; the dependent creature nevertheless retains the extraordinary status already established.

**External support:** Genesis 2:7 scholarship confirms the sequence “dust of the ground → divine breath → living being” and cautions against reading *nephesh chayyah* as a uniquely human separable soul, since related living-creature terminology also applies to animals. ([OUP Academic][2])

**External challenge:** Later biblical uses of dust can carry associations of mortality, frailty, humiliation, or death.

**Adjudication:** Those associations do not make “worthlessness” the lexical meaning of dust in Genesis 2:7, and they cannot erase Genesis 1's independently established image/dominion anchor.

**Claim boundary:** The claim does not define the image of God or provide a complete anthropology.

**Rejected overclaims:** “Dust means humans are insignificant.” “Image means humans are independent of created reality.”

**Methodology controls:** Wide-Angle Lens; Local Confidence; Anchored Inquiry; Noncompetitive Conjunction.

## CA-G2-05 — The Deliberately Established Place

**Claim:** The garden is a deliberately established human place characterized by abundant, multidimensional provision.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** God plants the garden, places the human there, causes trees to grow, describes them as desirable to sight and good for food, and provides water.

**Reasoning:** The garden is a particular location within a wider world rather than an exhaustive description of the planet. Its trees possess at least two explicitly disclosed dimensions of value: nutritional and visual.

**External support:** Academic translation/commentary recognizes the distinction between Eden, the garden, the outward-flowing river, and wider named geography; it also preserves the dual description of trees as delightful to look at and good for food. ([Bible Gateway][3])

**External challenge:** Some scholarship interprets Eden as divine garden, sanctuary, cosmic center, or source of the world's waters. The NABRE notes, for example, explicitly develop divine-garden and universal-river interpretations. ([Bible Gateway][3])

**Adjudication:** Those interpretations demonstrate the seriousness of the geography but exceed what our local investigation needs. The safer formulation remains **distinctive orienting position**, not proven cosmic or geographical center.

**Claim boundary:** We do not know the garden's complete purpose, exact modern location, or why the precious materials are disclosed.

**Rejected overclaims:** “Beauty is unnecessary.” “The precious materials are primarily economic.” “Eden has been proven the physical center of Earth.”

**Methodology controls:** Multidimensional Created Value; Effect ≠ Purpose; Smallest-Necessary Research.

## CA-G2-06 — Actionable Clarity

**Claim:** The boundary is communicated within broad permission and with actionable clarity.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 2:16 begins with “every tree” and the emphatic *akhol tokhel*, conventionally rendered “you may freely eat.” One tree is excepted in 2:17, the prohibited action is eating, and *mot tamut* communicates death emphatically. The infinitive-absolute constructions support the emphatic force of both permission and warning. ([NET Bible][6])

**Reasoning:** The human possesses enough information to distinguish conduct corresponding with God's communication from conduct crossing the boundary.

**External challenge:** Scholars dispute what “in the day” entails and how eating relates precisely to death.

**Adjudication:** Those disputes concern the **mechanics and timing of consequence**, not whether the actionable boundary was communicated.

**Claim boundary:** Actionable clarity does not mean exhaustive understanding.

**Rejected overclaims:** “God explained everything.” “Adam possessed exhaustive experiential knowledge of death.” “The precise death mechanism is settled.”

**Open questions:** Why this tree? Why this boundary? What precisely does *beyom* entail?

**Methodology controls:** Actionable Clarity; Undisclosed ≠ Withheld; Explanatory Incompleteness ≠ Actionable Ambiguity.

## CA-G2-07 — Conditions Favoring Compliance

**Claim:** The disclosed conditions favor informed compliance and provide no disclosed evidentiary basis for construing God's warning as malicious or deceptive.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** Food-bearing trees already exist; permission extends emphatically to every garden tree except one; the exception is identified; the consequence is communicated beforehand. No food scarcity or survival pressure requiring violation has been narrated. ([NET Bible][6])

**Reasoning:** The narrative gives the human abundant permitted alternatives, a narrow restriction, direct communication, and grave advance warning. These are descriptively favorable conditions for compliance.

**External challenge:** The NET note interprets the arrangement explicitly as a test of obedience. ([NET Bible][6]) Other interpreters dispute whether death is punishment, inherent consequence, mortality, or loss of access to life.

**Adjudication:** Even if “test” ultimately proves a useful explanatory category, **test ≠ enticement toward failure**. Genesis 2 supplies no psychology of temptation and no evidence that the prohibited tree was made uniquely irresistible.

**Claim boundary:** We do not claim that facilitating compliance was God's complete purpose.

**Rejected overclaims:** “The tree itself constitutes temptation.” “God intentionally entices the human toward failure.” “Severe consequence proves ill will.”

**Methodology controls:** Observable Condition ≠ Purpose; Availability ≠ Temptation; Severity ≠ Disproportionality; Unanswered Question ≠ Counterevidence.

## CA-G2-08 — Sovereignty–Agency Compatibility

**Claim:** Divine sovereignty and genuine human agency coexist without narrated conflict.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** God creates the animals and brings them to the human “to see what he would call them”; whatever the human calls each creature becomes its name. The decision is attributed to the human and has standing.

**External support:** Literary scholarship emphasizes the unusual prominence of naming in 2:19–20 and the fact that God brings the creatures while the human performs the naming. Hauser notes the repeated naming vocabulary and treats the episode as an extended human examination of the animals. ([eNotes][7]) Another interpretive tradition explicitly connects animal naming with the dominion granted in Genesis 1. ([Calvin University][8])

**External challenge:** Theological traditions disagree sharply over the philosophical relationship between divine sovereignty, foreknowledge, and human freedom.

**Adjudication:** Genesis 2 need not solve those philosophical systems. The narrower narrative relationship is sufficient: God remains Lord while the human's action is genuinely attributed to the human and allowed to stand.

**Claim boundary:** No claim about libertarianism, determinism, compatibilism, or exhaustive foreknowledge.

**Methodology controls:** Sovereignty–Agency Compatibility; Claim-Distance Control; Smallest-Necessary Research.

## CA-G2-09 — Agency Before Misuse

**Claim:** Human agency first appears as legitimate, consequential participation within God's order.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** The Lord initiates the animal encounter, the human exercises naming agency, and the resulting names stand. No rebellion or conflict is attached to this first sustained exercise of human choice.

**Reasoning:** Human volition is therefore positively established before any later misuse could appear.

**External support:** The narrative wording itself gives unusual weight to the human's naming decision—“whatever” he calls each living creature becomes its name. Literary analysis treats the naming process as significant rather than decorative. ([eNotes][7])

**External challenge:** Naming can be interpreted primarily as dominion/authority rather than as evidence concerning agency generally.

**Adjudication:** These are not competing explanations. Naming may exercise derivative authority precisely through genuine human agency. The claim does not universalize every human choice; it identifies the narrative's positive first occurrence.

**Claim boundary:** Later misuse remains fully open to later adjudication.

**Methodology controls:** Positive Priority; Consequential Creaturely Agency; Created Participation ≠ Independent Origination.

## CA-G2-10 — Male/Female Correspondence

**Claim:** The woman is presented as corresponding to the man within the image-bearing humanity already established in Genesis 1, not as a lesser human.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 1:27 already includes male and female within image-bearing humanity. Genesis 2 adds *ʿezer kenegdo*, the failure to find such correspondence among the animals, “this time/at last,” and “bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.”

**External support:** Linguistic research recognizes serious problems with translating *ʿezer kenegdo* as though *ʿezer* inherently encoded subordinate status and emphasizes the correspondence/facing dimension of *kenegdo*. ([Claremont Colleges Scholarship][9]) The Hebrew construction itself supports “corresponding to him/opposite him” rather than simple subordinate assistance. ([Fellowshipbook][4]) Jon Levenson, as quoted in discussion of *The Jewish Study Bible*, notes that creation after and from the man “need not imply” subordination. ([Mariottini Old Testament][10])

**External challenge:** Some traditions infer male authority from the sequence of formation and interpret Adam's “she shall be called” as another authority-bearing naming act. One such argument explicitly connects animal naming and the woman's designation to male headship. ([Calvin University][8])

**Adjudication:** The challenge does not overturn the prior Genesis 1:27 anchor or Genesis 2's correspondence/shared-flesh evidence. Moreover, interpreters have noted that 2:23 differs from the animal-naming formula and may function primarily as recognition of who she is rather than an identical act of dominion naming. ([Psephizo][11])

**Claim boundary:** This does not adjudicate every later biblical question concerning male/female roles.

**Rejected overclaims:** “Sequence proves inferiority.” “Derivation proves lesser image-bearing status.” “Woman's narrative silence proves reduced cognition.”

**Methodology controls:** Anchored Inquiry; Disclosure Asymmetry ≠ Ontological Asymmetry; Naming Act ≠ Single Fixed Function.

## CA-G2-11 — God Acts Against Aloneness

**Claim:** God acts against human aloneness toward corresponding human relationship.

**Status / confidence:** **SUPPORTED — HIGH**

**Textual evidence:** God identifies the human's being alone as “not good,” announces “I will make” a corresponding helper, and acts toward establishing that relationship. The animals do not resolve the correspondence condition; the woman does.

**External support:** The Hebrew grammar directly attaches *lo tov* to the human's being alone and immediately follows it with God's announced action. ([Fellowshipbook][4]) Literary analysis emphasizes that the animal sequence culminates in the explicit failure to find an *ʿezer kenegdo*, making the woman's later correspondence structurally consequential. ([eNotes][7])

**External challenge:** The episode is often interpreted as Adam discovering his own need through the animal sequence.

**Adjudication:** That may describe an important literary effect, but God's complete purpose for arranging the sequence is not necessary to this claim. God has already identified the condition before Adam's animal encounters.

**Claim boundary:** The claim does not say Adam was psychologically lonely, complained, or consciously recognized his condition before the woman appeared.

**Open question:** Why was Adam given this experiential sequence at all?

**Methodology controls:** Effect ≠ Purpose; Attentive Care; Interesting Question
