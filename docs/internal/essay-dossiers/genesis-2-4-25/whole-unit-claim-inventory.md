## E. External-argument architecture for those 12 rows

The public table should eventually include the extra column we previously established: **“External arguments / counterarguments + citations.”**

For example:

### CA-G2-06 — Actionable clarity

**Claim:**
The boundary is communicated within broad permission and with actionable clarity.

**Supporting argument:**
The human receives the permitted scope, specific exception, prohibited action, and emphatic consequence.

**External challenge:**
Scholars dispute the exact force of “in the day” and the mechanics of death.

**Adjudication:**
Those disputes affect explanation of the consequence, not whether the actionable boundary was communicated.

That distinction is exactly what the public audit should make visible.

### CA-G2-08 — Sovereignty and agency

**Claim:**
Divine sovereignty and genuine human agency coexist without narrated conflict.

**Supporting argument:**
God creates and brings the animals; Adam genuinely determines their names; the names stand.

**External challenge:**
Later philosophical frameworks disagree about how divine sovereignty and human freedom can coexist.

**Adjudication:**
Genesis 2 does not adjudicate those philosophical systems. The narrower narrative relationship is nevertheless observable.

### CA-G2-10 — Male/female correspondence

**Claim:**
Woman is presented as corresponding to man within image-bearing humanity rather than as a lesser human.

**Supporting argument:**
Genesis 1:27 includes male and female within image-bearing humanity; Genesis 2 adds *kenegdo*, animal contrast, shared flesh, and Adam's recognition.

**External challenge:**
Later interpreters have inferred hierarchy from sequence or derivation.

**Adjudication:**
Sequence and derivation alone do not overturn the prior image-bearing anchor or the correspondence language. Later relational structures must be adjudicated from their own evidence.

# F. Claim-distance warning for the essay

The eventual essay should **not try to carry every finding**.

Its likely spine is already visible:

> **Genesis 1 showed us the Creator. Genesis 2 lets us watch what that Creator's lordship looks like when exercised within sustained human relationships.**

Then the evidence can progressively show:

**Lord who gives**

→ **Lord who provides**

→ **Lord who permits and warns**

→ **Lord who makes room for consequential human agency**

→ **Lord who notices human condition**

→ **Lord who acts toward human relationship and unity**

The essay does **not** need to argue:

> God is love.

The evidence-proportional conclusion is more interesting precisely because it is narrower:

> **The Lord Genesis 2 reveals is consistently beneficent, and his beneficence takes increasingly relational forms: proactive provision, protective care, attentive care, and room for genuine creaturely participation.**

That is a substantial Character-of-God claim without borrowing a later canonical conclusion.
