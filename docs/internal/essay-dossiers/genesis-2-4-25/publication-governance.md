## 1. Authoritative Essay Draft — Three Fidelity Refinements

These three replacements should now be considered incorporated into the authoritative draft.

### Reader versus human evidence

Replace:

> This is the One who gave the human life.
> The One who established his place.
> The One who supplied food.

with:

> **The reader already knows this as the One who gave the human life, established his place, and supplied his food. The human himself has received the resulting life, place, provision, and command.**

This preserves the important distinction between **reader evidence** and **character evidence**.

### Consequential agency

Replace:

> The Lord can establish a world in which a creature's decisions genuinely matter.

with:

> **The Lord can establish situations in which a creature's decisions genuinely matter.**

This prevents a local demonstration from silently becoming a universal proposition.

### Wider-world materials

Replace:

> the wider created reality contains forms of value whose complete significance has not yet been explained.

with:

> **the wider created reality contains notable features whose complete significance has not yet been explained.**

“Multidimensional value” remains specifically grounded in the garden's **desirable-to-sight + good-for-food** trees.

With those changes:

> **AUTHORITATIVE PRE-PUBLICATION ESSAY DRAFT: FROZEN**

Further changes should be treated as explicit revisions rather than silent cleanup.

# 2. Citation Architecture

The essay should follow the accessibility model we established in the earlier Genesis essays:

### Main prose

Keep citations **light**.

The essay should not look like an academic paper.

Direct biblical quotations can identify the passage naturally. Technical claims requiring scholarship can use unobtrusive notes/links where necessary.

### Short bibliography

At the end:

> **Further Reading / Sources Consulted**

This should contain only sources that materially influenced consequential judgments—not every resource examined during investigation.

### Claim Audit

This is where citation density increases substantially.

Each public claim should expose:

* claim;
* status;
* concise rationale;
* external argument/counterargument;
* citations;
* link to full rationale.

### Argument Record

This is the deepest public evidence layer.

It should preserve the actual reasoning, competing interpretations, evidence boundaries, and adjudication.

So the architecture becomes:

> **Essay → Claim Audit → Argument Record → Authoritative DA**

Increasing depth without forcing every reader through every layer.

# 3. Recommended Short Bibliography

The bibliography should represent the actual scholarly dialogue that materially affected our adjudication.

I recommend keeping it approximately this size:

### Biblical text / lexical resources

* Hebrew Bible / Genesis 2:4–25.
* A major Hebrew lexicon for consequential terms such as *ʿafar*, *ʿavad*, *shamar*, *ʿezer*, *kenegdo*, *dabaq*, and the emphatic verbal constructions.
* Major translation comparison where translation materially affected interpretation.

### Commentaries / literary studies

* **Nahum M. Sarna, *Genesis: The Traditional Hebrew Text with the New JPS Translation*** — particularly useful for Hebrew literary relationships and the garden narrative.
* **Gordon J. Wenham, *Genesis 1–15*** — especially Genesis 2's structure, garden, *ʿezer kenegdo*, and one-flesh relationship.
* **Umberto Cassuto, *A Commentary on the Book of Genesis, Part I*** — especially literary connections across Genesis 2–3.
* **Terje Stordalen, “Man, Soil, Garden: Basic Plot in Genesis 2–3 Reconsidered”** — consequential for recognizing Genesis 2:5 as prospective/programmatic.
* **John L. McKenzie, “The Literary Characteristics of Genesis 2–3”** — useful for the literary movement toward the creation of woman as climax.
* **Sarah Turner-Smith, work on nakedness in Genesis 2:25** — important counterpressure against overreading “naked and not ashamed” through modern assumptions.

### Comparative/contextual scholarship

Use selectively for the earth-material/human-creation comparison and non-conflict observation. The essay itself probably does not need this discussion; the Claim Audit/Argument Records can.

The bibliography should **not** become evidence that every scholar listed agrees with our conclusions. It records serious dialogue.

# 6. Example Complete Argument Record

### CA-G2-06 — Actionable Clarity

**Claim**

> The boundary is communicated within broad permission and with actionable clarity.

**Status**

> SUPPORTED

**Confidence**

> HIGH

**Scope**

> Genesis 2:15–17 within accumulated Genesis 1:1–2:14 revealed history.

**Textual evidence**

God first grants broad permission:

> every tree of the garden may be eaten.

One tree is then excepted.

The relevant prohibited action is eating.

Death is communicated emphatically as the consequence.

**Accumulated evidence**

The garden has already been described as containing abundant food-bearing trees. No survival scarcity has been narrated.

**Reasoning**

The human therefore possesses sufficient information to distinguish behavior corresponding with God's communication from behavior crossing the boundary.

The narrative does not provide exhaustive explanation concerning the tree, boundary, death mechanism, or complete divine purpose.

That explanatory incompleteness does not make the required action unclear.

**External support**

Hebrew scholarship recognizes the emphatic construction behind “you may freely eat” and the emphatic force behind “you shall surely die.”

**External challenge**

Scholars disagree about the precise meaning of “in the day,” whether death becomes inevitable, whether access to the tree of life is decisive, and the exact causal/judicial relationship between eating and death.

**Adjudication**

Those disagreements concern **explanation of the consequence**, not whether the human was told which action was permitted, which was prohibited, and that the prohibited action carried a grave consequence.

**Claim boundary**

This claim does not establish:

* exhaustive understanding;
* exact death mechanics;
* complete divine purpose;
* philosophical moral responsibility beyond what the narrative supplies.

**Rejected overclaims**

> God explained everything.

> Adam had exhaustive experiential knowledge of death.

> The death mechanism is settled.

**Open questions**

Why this tree?

Why this boundary?

What precisely does “in the day” entail?

**Methodology controls**

Actionable Clarity
Curated Vector
Undisclosed ≠ Withheld
Explanatory Incompleteness ≠ Global Uncertainty
Local Confidence

That is the depth I recommend for all 12 records.

# 7. Claim-Audit Rationale Linking

The essay should not clutter paragraphs with “Claim CA-G2-06.”

Instead, relevant sections can offer a quiet link:

> **See the evidence and claim audit**

The Claim Audit then gives the table.

The table gives:

> **Read rationale**

The rationale gives the full Argument Record.

That preserves the common-reader essay while making the epistemic architecture transparent to anyone who wants it.
