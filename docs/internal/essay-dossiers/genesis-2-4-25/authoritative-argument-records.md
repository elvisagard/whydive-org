# Genesis 2:4–25 — Authoritative Argument Records

**Governance:** These records do not reopen adjudication. They document the evidence, external dialogue, boundaries, and rationale behind the already-approved public Claim Audit.

## CA-G2-01 — Grounded Lordship

**Claim:** The Creator's lordship is already grounded before Genesis 2 begins sustained human-divine interaction.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Scope:** Genesis 1:1–2:4, carried into 2:4–25.

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 1 consistently presents God as the originating actor who creates, differentiates, names, assigns functions, blesses, provides, evaluates, and delegates dominion. Genesis 2:4 identifies **YHWH Elohim** with the making of earth and heavens.

**Accumulated reasoning:** Genesis 2 does not narrate God acquiring jurisdiction over an independently existing world. Creatorship is therefore the elemental basis for the ownership/lordship inference: God made it; no prior claimant or authorizer is disclosed.

**External dialogue:** Source-critical scholarship strongly distinguishes the style, vocabulary, sequence, and traditional sources of Genesis 1:1–2:3 and 2:4ff. That is a genuine literary/compositional issue. It does not, however, remove the received text's explicit identification of YHWH Elohim as the maker of earth and heavens.

**Adjudication:** The claim concerns the literary/canonical sequence being studied, not common authorship of the two accounts.

**Claim boundary:** “Ownership” is a strong relational inference from creatorship, not a quotation of an abstract Genesis proposition about property law.

**Rejected overclaims:** Genesis 2 establishes God's lordship through conquest; God received ownership from another authority.

**Methodology controls:** Revealed History; Anchored Inquiry; Claim-Distance Control.

## CA-G2-02 — Beneficent Lordship

**Claim:** The LORD repeatedly exercises his authority beneficently toward the human.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** God forms and gives life (2:7), plants and places (2:8), supplies food and water (2:9–14), gives responsibility and broad permission (2:15–16), warns concerning death (2:17), identifies aloneness as not good and acts concerning it (2:18–23).

**Reasoning:** “Beneficent” describes the direction of these actions toward creaturely good without requiring a complete account of God's emotional state.

**External dialogue:** Scholarship commonly recognizes Eden as a setting of divine provision and Genesis 2:18 as the first explicit “not good,” focused on the human's condition.

**Adjudication:** The cumulative behavioral evidence is stronger than any single occurrence.

**Claim boundary:** Beneficence does not yet require the stronger canonical proposition **“God is love.”**

**Rejected overclaims:** Genesis 2 explicitly defines God as love; every action's complete purpose is known.

**Methodology controls:** Cumulative Evidence; Local Confidence; Character/Nature/Reality distinction.

## CA-G2-03 — Proactive, Protective, and Attentive Care

**Claim:** God's beneficence includes proactive provision, protective warning, and attentive care.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Provision precedes request; the death warning precedes violation; God identifies aloneness before a human complaint is narrated.

**Reasoning:** These categories describe three distinguishable behavioral forms of beneficence:

* **proactive:** benefit before request;
* **protective:** actionable warning before grave consequence;
* **attentive:** recognition and response to a negatively appraised condition.

**External challenge:** One could resist “care” as an emotional inference. But the claim does not depend on reconstructing God's emotion. Advance warning relevant to avoiding harm and action directed toward a negatively appraised human condition are care-like behaviors observable within the narrative.

**Adjudication:** Protective and attentive care are warranted behaviorally.

**Claim boundary:** No claim is made concerning exhaustive divine psychology.

**Methodology controls:** Effect ≠ Purpose; Character from Behavior; Claim-Distance Control.

## CA-G2-04 — Dignity and Dependence

**Claim:** Human dignity and creaturely dependence are simultaneously maintained.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 1:26–28 gives image, likeness, blessing, and dominion. Genesis 2:7 gives dust from the ground, divine formation, divine breath, and received life.

**External dialogue:** Scholarship widely recognizes the *adam/adamah* wordplay and earth-derived human identity. Studies of *ʿafar* also note its literal earth/dust sense and later associations with human frailty and mortality.

**Adjudication:** Neither disclosure cancels the other. Dust does not erase image-bearing status; image-bearing status does not erase creaturely derivation and received life.

**Claim boundary:** *ʿAfar* does not lexically mean “dependence upon God.” Dependence emerges from the narrated relationship among material, divine forming, breath, and life.

**Rejected overclaims:** Dust = worthlessness; image = independence; Genesis 2:7 establishes a separately inserted immortal soul.

**Methodology controls:** Wide-Angle Lens; Local Confidence; Noncompetitive Conjunction.

## CA-G2-05 — The Deliberately Established Place

**Claim:** The garden is a deliberately established human place characterized by abundant, multidimensional provision.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** God plants the garden, places the human there, causes trees to grow, provides food-bearing and visually desirable trees, and the garden is watered.

**External dialogue:** Commentators regularly recognize both aesthetic and nutritional dimensions in “pleasant/desirable to sight and good for food.” Scholarship also distinguishes Eden, the garden in Eden, and the wider geographical world. Attempts to reconstruct all four rivers geographically remain contested, especially Pishon and Gihon.

**Adjudication:** The garden is differentiated space established by divine action. Its relationship to the wider world is real; its complete geographical/theological function remains unresolved.

**Claim boundary:** Use **“distinctive orienting position”** rather than “center.” Do not classify visual desirability as unnecessary.

**Rejected overclaims:** Eden is proven to be the geographical/cosmic center; precious materials are primarily economic resources; beauty is superfluous decoration.

**Methodology controls:** Curated Vector; Effect ≠ Purpose; Smallest-Necessary Research.

---

## CA-G2-06 — Actionable Clarity

**Claim:** The boundary is communicated within broad permission and with actionable clarity.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 2:16–17 gives broad permission, one identified exception, the prohibited action, and an emphatically stated death consequence.

**Lexical evidence:** The Hebrew infinitive-absolute constructions intensify both permission (*ʾakhol tokhel*) and warning (*mot tamut*). Scholarly discussion recognizes the emphatic force while debating the precise temporal/causal interpretation of death.

**External challenge:** “In the day” and the fact that the humans do not immediately collapse dead have generated multiple interpretations: immediacy, inevitability, mortality, loss of access to life, or idiomatic temporal force.

**Adjudication:** These disagreements affect the **explanation of the consequence**, not whether the actionable boundary is communicated.

**Claim boundary:** Actionable clarity ≠ exhaustive explanation.

**Rejected overclaims:** Adam understood the complete metaphysics of death; Genesis settles the exact death mechanism.

**Methodology controls:** Actionable Clarity; Undisclosed ≠ Withheld; Local Confidence.

## CA-G2-07 — Conditions Favoring Compliance

**Claim:** The disclosed conditions favor informed compliance and provide no disclosed evidentiary basis for construing God's warning as malicious or deceptive.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Food provision precedes the boundary; permission is broad; one tree is excepted; warning is direct and emphatic; no survival scarcity is narrated; no statement makes the prohibited tree uniquely attractive relative to permitted alternatives.

**External dialogue:** Literary readings regularly emphasize the breadth of “every tree” before the exception and recognize 2:16–17 as the baseline against which the serpent's later representation of God's words can be compared.

**Adjudication:** Favorable conditions are observable without knowing God's complete purpose in arranging them.

**Claim boundary:** “No disclosed basis for distrust” is bounded to the evidence supplied through 2:17; it is not a metaphysical claim about every conceivable undisclosed fact.

**Rejected overclaims:** The tree's availability itself constitutes temptation; God is depicted enticing the human toward failure; severe consequence proves ill will.

**Methodology controls:** Observable Condition ≠ Purpose; Availability ≠ Temptation; Unanswered Question ≠ Counterevidence.

## CA-G2-08 — Sovereignty–Agency Compatibility

**Claim:** Divine sovereignty and genuine human agency coexist without narrated conflict.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** God forms and brings the animals; Adam determines what to call them; “whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name.”

**External dialogue:** Scholarship frequently reads animal naming as an expression of human dominion/authority and recognizes naming as consequential in ancient biblical contexts.

**Reasoning:** God's originating authority remains intact while Adam's action is genuinely attributed to Adam and permitted to have standing.

**Adjudication:** Genesis depicts coexistence rather than competition.

**Claim boundary:** The passage does not adjudicate libertarian free will, compatibilism, determinism, or exhaustive foreknowledge.

**Rejected overclaims:** Adam's naming is merely theatrical; God's sovereignty requires Adam's decisions to be unreal.

**Methodology controls:** Sovereignty–Agency Compatibility; Consequential Creaturely Agency.

---

## CA-G2-09 — Positive Priority of Human Agency

**Claim:** Human agency first appears as legitimate, consequential participation within God's order.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Adam's naming occurs at God's initiative but is genuinely Adam's action; the resulting names stand.

**Reasoning:** The first sustained depiction of human discretionary action is not rebellion. It is authorized participation.

**External dialogue:** Dominion readings of the naming episode reinforce its connection with Genesis 1:26–28 rather than treating it as an act hostile to divine authority.

**Adjudication:** Later misuse of agency cannot retroactively become agency's original definition.

**Claim boundary:** This does not establish that every exercise of human choice is morally good.

**Rejected overclaims:** Human freedom enters the story as the problem; creaturely agency necessarily threatens divine sovereignty.

**Methodology controls:** Positive Priority; First-Occurrence Priority.

## CA-G2-10 — Male/Female Correspondence

**Claim:** The woman is presented as corresponding to the man within the image-bearing humanity already established in Genesis 1, not as a lesser human.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** Genesis 1:27 includes male and female in image-bearing humanity. Genesis 2 adds *ʿezer kenegdo*, failure of animal correspondence, “this time,” and “bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.”

**Lexical/external dialogue:** Scholarship widely cautions that *ʿezer* does not lexically mean subordinate assistant; the noun can describe strong aid, including divine help. *Kenegdo* conveys correspondence/opposite-facing/suitability.

**External challenge:** Some theological traditions infer hierarchy from male-first sequence, derivation, or naming.

**Adjudication:** Sequence and derivation do not overturn the already-established image-bearing status or the local emphasis on correspondence and shared flesh.

**Claim boundary:** The claim does not settle every later biblical question concerning gender roles.

**Rejected overclaims:** Woman is less image-bearing; derivation itself establishes inferiority; *ʿezer* means servant.

**Methodology controls:** Revelatory Anchor; Disclosure Asymmetry ≠ Ontological Asymmetry; Local Confidence.

---

## CA-G2-11 — God Acts Against Aloneness

**Claim:** God acts against human aloneness toward corresponding human relationship.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — HIGH

**Textual evidence:** God declares the human's aloneness “not good,” announces “I will make…,” and subsequently establishes the corresponding human relationship.

**External dialogue:** Commentaries commonly identify 2:18 as the first “not good” after Genesis 1's repeated positive evaluations and understand *ʿezer kenegdo* as addressing the solitary human condition.

**Reasoning:** The negatively appraised condition is relational; God's enacted response is relational.

**Adjudication:** This supports attentive care without requiring an invented psychology of Adamic loneliness.

**Claim boundary:** “Alone” is textual; “lonely” is not.

**Rejected overclaims:** Adam explicitly complained; God acts only after human request.

**Methodology controls:** Character from Behavior; Textual Observation ≠ Psychological Reconstruction.

---

## CA-G2-12 — Human Relational Unity as Culmination

**Claim:** Human relational unity is the literary culmination of Genesis 2:4–25, while later rupture must not be projected backward into the original relationship.

**Status:** SUPPORTED — STRONG

**Textual evidence:** Alone/not good → no animal counterpart → woman → “this time” → bone/flesh → cling → one flesh → naked/not ashamed.

**External support:** Literary scholarship has explicitly described the creation of woman/one-flesh relationship as the climax toward which Genesis 2 moves. *Dabaq* expresses strong attachment, while “one flesh” is widely understood as more than merely a reference to intercourse.

**External counterpressure:** Sarah Turner-Smith cautions that interpreters may over-romanticize Genesis 2:25 by importing modern assumptions about nakedness, innocence, and idyllic intimacy.

**Adjudication:** The narrow baseline remains secure:

> nakedness is present; shame is absent.

The culmination claim rests primarily on the movement from aloneness to correspondence and one-flesh union, not on an inflated psychology of 2:25.

**Claim boundary:** Culmination ≠ ultimate purpose of creation. “Not ashamed” ≠ exhaustive psychological transparency.

**Rejected overclaims:** one flesh means only intercourse; Genesis 2:25 proves a complete psychology of perfect intimacy; everything in creation exists for marriage.

**Methodology controls:** Positive Priority; Culmination ≠ Purpose; Claim-Distance Control.
