# Genesis 3:1–7 — Formal Claim Adjudication & Argument Record

**Status:** Permanent whole-unit adjudication record  
**Source basis:** Seven frozen Genesis 3:1–7 verse dossiers + frozen Genesis 3:1–7 Synthesis Dossier  
**Hard information boundary:** **Genesis 3:7. Genesis 3:8+ is excluded.**  
**Governing question:** **What does the evidence actually authorize?**

## Record Purpose
This record converts the completed synthesis into durable claim-level adjudications. Each major claim is classified by evidence type, tested against alternatives, bounded against overclaiming, and assigned a public-audit disposition.

Statuses used:
- **TEXTUAL FINDING**
- **HIGH-CONFIDENCE FINDING**
- **STRONG ANALYTICAL FINDING**
- **STRONG INFERENCE**
- **STRONG PURPOSE HYPOTHESIS**
- **METHODOLOGICAL FINDING**
- **NOT AUTHORIZED / REJECTED**
- **OPEN**

Public-audit dispositions:
- **CORE** — suitable as a central public claim.
- **SUPPORTING** — useful supporting claim.
- **QUALIFIED** — usable publicly only with explicit boundary.
- **METHOD** — belongs primarily in methodology/audit explanation.
- **HOLD** — preserve but do not make a public claim yet.

# A. Human Evidentiary Position and the Woman’s Trajectory

## FCA-01 — The woman’s first response to the serpent is corrective

**Claim:** The woman initially responds to the serpent’s distorted representation by correcting it rather than capitulating.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE FINDING  
**Evidence:** 3:1 foregrounds prohibition broadly; 3:2 answers, “We may eat,” restoring the general permission already disclosed.  
**Counterargument:** Her engagement itself may show susceptibility.  
**Response:** Engagement is not evidence of capitulation. Her disclosed content corrects the serpent’s scope.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Boundary:** Does not establish her complete internal state or immunity to later influence.  
**Rationale:** The first recorded human response to competing representation preserves God’s provision.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-02 — The woman is not demonstrated cognitively compromised through 3:3

**Claim:** Genesis 3:1–3 does not portray the woman as confused, gullible, forgetful, intellectually inferior, or already deteriorating.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE NEGATIVE FINDING  
**Evidence:** She understands the question, retrieves relevant prior information, corrects scope, represents provision corporately, identifies the boundary, and associates violation with death.  
**Counterargument:** “Do not touch” and altered death wording prove deterioration.  
**Response:** “Do not touch” has unresolved provenance; non-verbatim wording does not independently establish semantic distortion or weakened certainty.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Boundary:** Does not prove exhaustive accuracy of every detail she knows.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-03 — Temptation has a disclosed onset

**Claim:** Human temptation is not demonstrated merely by availability, conversation, or inducement; attraction first becomes disclosed in 3:6.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE FINDING + METHODOLOGICAL FINDING  
**Evidence:** Through 3:5 no attraction is narrated. In 3:6 the tree becomes good for food, desirable to the eyes, and desirable in relation to knowledge/wisdom gain.  
**Counterargument:** The serpent’s presence and prohibited option already constitute temptation.  
**Response:** Those establish opportunity and inducement, not demonstrated attractive pull in the recipient.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Boundary:** Uses the study’s operational definition of temptation as evidenced attraction/enticement.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE / METHOD.**

# B. Serpent Communication and Evidentiary Status

## FCA-04 — The serpent’s discourse progressively escalates

**Claim:** The serpent moves from distortion/reframing to direct contradiction to alternative explanation and prospective gain.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE LITERARY FINDING  
**Evidence:** 3:1 → 3:4 → 3:5 sequence.  
**Counterargument:** These may simply be independent statements.  
**Response:** They concern the same boundary and progressively increase the challenge to God’s disclosed account.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-05 — The serpent supplies counterclaim, not counterevidence

**Claim:** Before the humans act, the serpent gives consequential counterclaims but no disclosed evidence overturning God’s reliability or warning.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE EPISTEMIC FINDING  
**Evidence:** No demonstration, witness, safe-eating example, verified prior prediction, evidence of divine unreliability, or disclosed epistemic provenance.  
**Counterargument:** The serpent’s explanation in 3:5 is itself evidence.  
**Response:** Explanation adds a model; it does not independently validate the model.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Boundary:** Does not establish that the serpent possesses no evidence outside narrated disclosure.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-06 — God and serpent have radically unequal disclosed evidentiary histories

**Claim:** The humans do not face two informational authorities with equivalent narrated reliability histories.

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE SYNTHESIS FINDING  
**Evidence:** God: creation, provision, blessing, relationship, speech/reality correspondence, instruction. Serpent: distorted framing, contradiction, unsupported claimed knowledge; no disclosed comparable history.  
**Counterargument:** The narrative may omit prior serpent-human encounters.  
**Response:** Possible prior encounters cannot be converted into a reliability history the narrative does not supply.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High within disclosed evidence.  
**Boundary:** “Disclosed” must remain explicit.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-07 — The serpent offers interpretation without alternative provision

**Claim:** The serpent supplies another account of God’s reality but no alternative material world, provision, or demonstrated beneficent relationship.

**Classification:** STRONG ANALYTICAL FINDING  
**Evidence:** The serpent creates/provides none of the garden, food, bodies, capacities, vegetation, or post-crossing resources.  
**Counterargument:** Communication itself is a provision of information.  
**Response:** Yes; the claim concerns material/relational provision and evidentiary history, not absence of communicative contribution.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT WITH BOUNDARY.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

# C. Influence, Interpretation, and Agency

**Classification:** HIGH-CONFIDENCE FINDING

**Counterargument:** Undisclosed coercion is possible.  
**Response:** Possibility is not evidence.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**

**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

**Evidence:** Woman corrects, evaluates, takes, eats; humans perceive, know, construct, cover.

**Response:** Influence is real, but the narrative continues attributing perception, evaluation, and action to the humans.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Boundary:** Does not adjudicate later philosophical free-will systems.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

**Evidence:** Serpent claims remain evidentially unsupported while becoming behaviorally consequential in 3:6.  
**Counterargument:** Behavioral uptake might imply perceived evidence.  
**Response:** Perceived persuasiveness is not disclosed evidentiary warrant.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE / METHOD.**

**Response:** None are narrated; the explanatory burden cannot be shifted to invented changes.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** High.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE.**

## FCA-18 — Shame is relevant but not explicitly named in 3:7

**Claim:** Shame is strongly implicated by the 2:25/3:7 contrast, while the secure observation is that nakedness now requires corrective/concealing action for the humans.

**Classification:** STRONG LITERARY INFERENCE  
**Evidence:** “not ashamed” baseline; post-crossing covering.  
**Counterargument:** Covering may represent maturation, sexuality, vulnerability, or culture.  
**Response:** Those alternatives do not overturn changed problematic/concealing response; they prevent over-specifying the emotion.

**Claim:** Correct prediction that a changed condition will occur does not establish faithful characterization of what that condition will mean when lived.

**Classification:** STRONG REPRESENTATIONAL / METHODOLOGICAL FINDING

**Counterargument:** The serpent did not promise emotional ease.  
**Response:** Correct; the finding does not require an explicit promise of ease. It distinguishes predicted features from the significance/framing of the condition as prospective gain.  
**Adjudication:** **ACCEPT.**  
**Confidence:** Strong.  
**Public-audit disposition:** **CORE / METHOD.**

# H. Methodological Adjudication

## MCA-01 — Inducement ≠ Temptation
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
**Rule:** Information capable of attracting a person does not establish temptation until attraction is evidenced in the recipient.

## MCA-02 — Propositional Correspondence ≠ Representational Faithfulness
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
**Rule:** Corresponding propositions are necessary evidence but do not alone adjudicate the complete faithfulness of a communicative representation.

## MCA-03 — Technical Accuracy ≠ Truthfulness of the Communicative Act
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE WITH CONTROL.**  
Evaluate framing, consequential omission, provenance, uptake, and resulting reality alongside individual propositions.

## MCA-04 — Experiential Acquisition Audit
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
When knowledge is acquired through action, test whether it becomes manifest through participation in or changed lived relationship to the reality known. Do not force experiential meaning lexically.

## MCA-05 — Replicated Interpretive Action
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE WITH NARROWING.**  
Influence may operate not merely by transferring conclusions but by reproducing an interpretive architecture in which existing realities are reorganized into altered relationships of meaning. Conscious imitation requires separate evidence.

## MCA-06 — Knowledge Asymmetry ≠ Epistemic Authority
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
Claiming inaccessible information does not establish reliability or authority.

## MCA-07 — Value ≠ Legitimate Means of Acquisition
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
The value of a capacity or knowledge does not establish that every means of obtaining it is legitimate or beneficial.

## MCA-08 — Narrative Sufficiency Before Technical Expansion
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
Before expanding into specialist complexity, ask whether disclosed narrative relationships already provide sufficient evidence for responsible adjudication.

## MCA-09 — Technical Complexity ≠ Interpretive Necessity
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
Genuine lexical/textual/scholarly complexity does not automatically require resolution for the claim presently under adjudication.

## MCA-10 — Specialist Testing, Not Specialist Preemption
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**  
Specialist study should test, refine, challenge, or increase resolution of first-reader recognition when materially relevant; it should not preempt narratively sufficient recognition merely because technical complexity is available.

**Diagnostic:** **Do not let a harder question replace the question the text has already answered.**

**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**

## MCA-12 — Redirected Agency
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**

## MCA-13 — Outcome Prediction ≠ Outcome Characterization
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**

## MCA-14 — Experienced Meaning Can Change Without Material Change
**Disposition:** **PROMOTE.**

## MCA-15 — Observation Before Emotional Explanation

## MCA-16 — Remedy Status Requires Separate Adjudication

# K. Whole-Unit Argument Map

## Argument 1 — Competing representation becomes behaviorally consequential without first gaining evidentiary parity
1. God enters Genesis 3 with an accumulated disclosed history of provision and speech/reality correspondence.
2. The serpent’s opening distorts the scope of God’s provision.
3. The woman initially corrects that distortion.
4. The serpent escalates to contradiction and prospective gain without disclosed counterevidence/provenance.
5. The woman’s evaluation changes only later, in 3:6.
6. The serpent’s representation becomes behaviorally influential.
7. Therefore behavioral influence is distinguishable from evidentiary authorization.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED.**

## Argument 2 — Interpretation can reorganize lived relationship to unchanged reality
1. The tree is not narrated as materially changing.
2. The woman’s evaluation of it changes after the conversation.
3. Nakedness exists before the crossing without shame.
4. After crossing, the humans know/experience nakedness as requiring concealment.
5. Therefore materially unchanged realities can acquire altered experienced meaning through changed interpretation.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED.**

## Argument 3 — Human agency remains under influence
1. The woman demonstrates agency while correcting the serpent.
2. She later demonstrates agency while evaluating, taking, and eating.
3. The humans demonstrate agency while constructing coverings.
4. No narrated physical coercion replaces their action.
5. Therefore influence and genuine human agency coexist; direction changes without agency disappearing.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED.**

## Argument 4 — The serpent’s technical correspondences do not settle representational faithfulness
1. The serpent predicts opened eyes and knowing.
2. Both occur.
3. The prospective representation frames the changed state as desirable gain/elevation.
4. The first lived manifestation includes an unchanged condition becoming something humans seek to conceal.
5. Therefore outcome prediction and propositional correspondence must be distinguished from characterization of the outcome as lived.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED WITH CONTINUING BOUNDARY.**

## Argument 5 — The boundary’s protective effect is stronger than the claim about protective purpose
1. The post-crossing experiential condition is absent before crossing.
2. It appears after the prohibited action.
3. The boundary therefore stood between the humans and entry into that condition.
4. God’s prior portrait supports, but does not prove, protective intent.
5. Therefore barrier/protective effect is high confidence; protective purpose remains a strong hypothesis.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED WITH EFFECT/PURPOSE DISTINCTION.**

## Argument 6 — Changed human perception does not overturn the prior portrait of creation or Creator
1. Nakedness exists pre-crossing without shame.
2. No new bodily defect is narrated.
3. Post-crossing humans treat nakedness as requiring concealment.
4. Their changed perception therefore cannot itself establish that the prior created condition was defective.
5. Nothing before the human decision overturns God’s accumulated provision/reliability.

**Adjudication:** **SUSTAINED.**
