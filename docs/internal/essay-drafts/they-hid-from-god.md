# They Hid From God

Something has changed.

The man and his wife hear the LORD God moving in the garden.

And they hide.

If you already know this story, their response may not seem surprising. They ate from the tree God had told them not to eat from. Their eyes were opened. They realized they were naked. They made coverings for themselves.

Now God is present in the garden, and they hide among the trees.

It is easy to think we already know why.

Maybe they are afraid.

Maybe they are ashamed.

Maybe they expect punishment.

But Genesis 3:8 has not told us any of those things yet.

At this point in the story, we know what the humans **do**.

We do not yet know why they do it.

That leaves us with something worth watching.

## Something Has Changed

This is the first time Genesis shows the humans hiding from God.

Before eating from the tree, they had already received much from him. God created them. He provided food. He gave them work. He allowed them to make real choices. He established a boundary and told them the consequence of crossing it.

Then they crossed it.

Now they hear him in the garden and hide.

The change in their behavior is clear.

The humans withdraw from God.

But Genesis has not yet shown God withdrawing from them.

God is still part of the story. The humans have crossed the boundary he established, but his involvement with them has not disappeared.

We should not make that observation say more than it does.

It does not yet prove that God has forgiven them.

It does not prove that he is being gentle.

It does not prove that he is angry.

It does not tell us why he is moving in the garden.

It tells us something simpler.

The humans have withdrawn from God.

God has not yet been shown withdrawing from them.

## Look at Where They Hide

The humans hide among the trees.

We have seen these trees before.

The garden was God's provision. Earlier in Genesis, God caused trees to grow there that were pleasant to look at and good for food.

Now the trees become the place where the humans hide from the One who provided the garden.

We should be careful here too.

Genesis does not tell us that they chose the trees because they wanted to use God's own gifts against him. That would require us to know what they were thinking.

We do not.

What we can see is enough.

Something that had already been part of God's provision is now part of a very different relational scene.

The trees have not changed.

The humans' relationship to God has.

## Who Are They Hiding From?

This may be the more important question.

The humans are not hiding from someone who has just entered the story.

They are hiding from the LORD God.

We have been watching him.

He created.

He provided.

He communicated.

He gave the humans meaningful things to do.

He allowed the man to name the animals.

He provided the garden and its food.

He established a boundary.

He warned them about the consequence of crossing it.

None of that disappeared when the humans ate from the tree.

This matters because we can easily read Genesis 3:8 as though God has suddenly become only the authority whose rule they broke.

But that is not the complete history Genesis has given us.

The God connected to the boundary is also the God connected to the garden.

The God who warned is also the God who provided.

The God from whom they hide is the God we have already been watching.

That history does not tell us exactly what God will do next.

It does mean that we should not pretend we know nothing about the Person who is present.

## Their Choices Have Not Disappeared

There is something else worth noticing.

The humans are still making choices.

They had already made coverings for themselves.

Now they hear God, and they hide.

Crossing God's boundary did not turn them into people who could no longer act.

Even with God perceptibly present in the garden, they remain able to respond.

And their response is concealment.

We do not have to know exactly what God's presence sounded or felt like to recognize that.

Some translations describe the setting as the cool or breeze of the day. There are questions about exactly how the ancient wording should be understood. Scholars have proposed different possibilities.

Those questions matter when we try to describe the scene precisely.

But they do not erase what remains clear.

The humans perceive YHWH God in the garden.

They remain capable of acting.

And they hide.

## What Does Their Hiding Tell Us?

Now we come to a harder question.

What does their hiding tell us about **God**?

Suppose someone sees a particular person enter a room and immediately leaves.

That response tells you something.

At the very least, the person who left did not want to remain there.

You might also begin asking why.

Was the person afraid?

Angry?

Embarrassed?

Trying to avoid a difficult conversation?

Those are reasonable questions.

But notice what you cannot do.

You cannot look only at the person who left and know the complete character of the person who entered.

The response may tell you what the first person expects.

It does not prove that the expectation is correct.

That distinction becomes important in Genesis 3:8.

The humans hide from God.

Their hiding tells us something real about them and about their changed relationship with him.

But their hiding cannot, by itself, tell us what God is like.

It may show us something about what they now expect from him.

That is not the same thing.

We still have to watch God.

## What Someone Thinks About God Is Not Yet God's Character

This study has given us a useful way to describe the difference:

**Perceived Character is not the same as Demonstrated Character.**

That phrase needs some explanation.

*Perceived character* is what someone believes or expects another person to be like.

*Demonstrated character* is what that person actually shows through words, actions, choices, and relationships.

The two can match.

They can also be different.

Genesis 3:8 does not yet tell us which is true here.

That is important.

We should not decide that the humans have misunderstood God simply because they hide.

But we should not decide that their hiding proves God is someone they need to hide from either.

Their response cannot settle the question.

God's behavior must be allowed to speak for God.

## Let God's Behavior Earn the Adjective

This becomes difficult because we like adjectives.

We want to know whether God is angry.

Or gentle.

Or merciful.

Or loving.

Or hostile.

Or forgiving.

Those words matter because they describe character.

But Genesis 3:8 has not yet given us enough evidence to choose one of them as the explanation for God's behavior in this verse.

We know that God remains involved.

We know that he is perceptible in the garden.

We know that the humans hide from him.

We know that no post-transgression punishment from God has yet been narrated before they hide.

But continued involvement is not automatically forgiveness.

Presence is not automatically gentleness.

And human hiding is not automatically evidence of divine anger.

So we need to let God's behavior earn the adjective.

That means we keep watching what he actually says and does before deciding what his actions reveal about his character.

This protects us from two opposite mistakes.

We do not need to defend God before seeing what he does.

We also do not need to accuse him before seeing what he does.

We can keep watching.

## What Do You Expect God to Do Next?

This is a good place to stop reading.

Really.

Do not move into the next verse yet.

The humans have crossed the boundary.

They have realized they are naked.

They have made coverings.

They have heard God in the garden.

They have hidden among the trees.

God has not yet spoken to them after what happened.

So what do you expect him to do?

Try to answer before reading the next verse.

Maybe you expect God to punish them immediately.

Maybe you expect anger.

Maybe you expect rejection.

Maybe you expect compassion.

Maybe you expect him to demand an explanation.

Maybe you expect something completely different.

Now ask another question:

**What made you expect that?**

Was it something Genesis has already shown you?

Was it something you learned in church?

Was it the way this story was taught to you as a child?

Was it another Bible passage?

Was it your experience with parents, teachers, pastors, or other people who had authority over you?

Was it something about your own personality?

Was it an experience that taught you what happens when you disappoint someone with power?

Those experiences can affect what we notice and what we expect.

They are worth paying attention to.

But they are not automatically evidence about God.

Genesis has given us a chance to notice the difference.

## We Have Seen What the Humans Do

At the end of Genesis 3:8, we know something we did not know before.

When the humans become aware of God's presence after crossing his boundary, they hide.

That is real evidence about their changed relationship with him.

But we still do not know whether the God they expect is the God who will actually respond.

We should not answer that question for God.

We have seen what the humans do.

Now we need to see what God does.

**Keep watching him.**
