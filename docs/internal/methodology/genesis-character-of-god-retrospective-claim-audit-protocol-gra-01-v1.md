
# Genesis Character-of-God Retrospective Claim-Audit Protocol

## GRA-01 — Version 1.0

### Repository Governance Note

This document is the controlling protocol for future retrospective Genesis essay audits and their implementation. Do not modify it as part of an individual essay update. Substantive methodological changes should be handled as governed protocol revisions under the version-governance rules below.

### Status

**Governed methodology — v1.0**

### Purpose

This protocol governs the retrospective auditing of already-drafted or already-published essays in the **Genesis Character-of-God** study.

Its purpose is not to prove that an essay was right.

Its purpose is to reconstruct the investigation honestly and determine:

> **What does the evidence presently authorize us to say?**

The protocol preserves:

* findings that survived scrutiny;
* strong but inferential judgments;
* translation sensitivity;
* competing interpretations;
* counterevidence;
* claims that weakened;
* claims that were revised;
* unresolved questions;
* later discoveries;
* the evidence trail behind present judgments.

The resulting publication architecture is:

> **Essay — Encounter**
> ↓
> **Claim Audit — Present Judgment**
> ↓
> **Argument Records — Why We Hold It**
> ↓
> **Revision / Emergent / Open-Question Records — How Knowledge Changed**
> ↓
> **Evidence — Text, Language, Scholarship, Canonical Correspondence**

---

# 1. Governing Principle

Every audit is governed by one question:

> **What does the evidence actually authorize?**

Not:

> What conclusion would make the essay stronger?

Not:

> What conclusion fits our theology best?

Not:

> What conclusion is most relationally useful?

Not:

> What did we already publish?

Publication does not confer authorization.

Attractiveness does not confer authorization.

Usefulness does not confer authorization.

Tradition does not automatically confer authorization.

The audit asks what survived the investigation and at what confidence.

---

# 2. Retrospective Auditing Is Reconstruction, Not Re-creation

The first task is to reconstruct the investigation that actually occurred.

Recover, where available:

* original observations;
* early hypotheses;
* user insights;
* assistant proposals;
* source-language investigation;
* translation comparisons;
* external thinkers;
* counterarguments;
* alternative interpretations;
* discarded claims;
* revised claims;
* later methodological corrections;
* manuscript drafts;
* published prose.

Do **not** clean the history before auditing it.

A retrospective audit must not manufacture an idealized research process that never happened.

---

# 3. The Published Essay Is Not the Evidence Base

Do not begin by assuming:

> published claim → find evidence supporting it.

Instead:

> **reconstruct evidence → adjudicate claim → compare published claim afterward.**

This ordering is mandatory.

Otherwise the audit becomes post-publication advocacy.

---

# 4. Build the Master Claim Inventory

Before selecting public claims, identify all consequential claims generated during the investigation.

Include claims that were:

* accepted;
* weakened;
* rejected;
* unresolved;
* later corrected;
* never published;
* published but subsequently questioned.

The inventory should normally contain **more claims than the eventual public Claim Audit**.

Suggested internal fields:

| Field                | Function                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Claim ID             | Stable internal identity          |
| Claim                | Actual proposition                |
| Origin               | Where/when it emerged             |
| Evidence type        | Textual, lexical, canonical, etc. |
| Current confidence   | Provisional status                |
| Publication location | Essay/movement                    |
| Public candidate?    | Yes/No                            |
| Notes                | Complications/provenance          |

---

# 5. Cluster Claims Before Adjudicating

Claims should be audited in conceptual clusters rather than randomly.

Typical clusters may include:

* lexical questions;
* narrative sequence;
* darkness/chaos/order;
* divine action;
* reader effect;
* theological inference;
* canonical correspondence;
* relational praxis.

This allows neighboring claims to be compared without allowing uncertainty in one claim to contaminate another.

---

# 6. Local Confidence Rule

A foundational rule established during the Genesis 1:1–2 investigation is:

> **Uncertainty should remain attached to the claim whose evidence warrants it.**

If one word is translation-sensitive, that does not automatically make:

* the entire verse uncertain;
* neighboring observations uncertain;
* unrelated theological claims uncertain.

Likewise, confidence in one observation does not automatically strengthen adjacent claims.

Confidence is **local**.

---

# 7. Distinguish Evidence Layers

Every consequential claim should be classified according to the kind of reasoning supporting it.

At minimum distinguish:

### Textual given

What the passage directly places before the reader.

### Literary observation

What follows from structure, sequence, selection, repetition, contrast, or narrative development.

### Source-language finding

What Hebrew, Greek, lexical range, syntax, or translation comparison contributes.

### Strong inference

A conclusion well supported by the evidence but not directly stated.

### Reader-effect inference

What the demonstrable form of the text makes available for the reader to notice, anticipate, question, or experience.

### Methodological application

A reading practice generated by the investigation.

### Theological synthesis

A larger conclusion drawing upon the passage plus broader theological premises.

### Relational/praxis implication

What the adjudicated recognition may mean for relationship with God or lived response.

These categories must not be silently collapsed.

---

# 8. Effect Is Not Automatically Purpose

This is a hard methodological rule.

A text may demonstrably:

* direct attention;
* delay information;
* create expectation;
* present scene before definition;
* foreground one reality before another.

Those are observable effects.

They do **not automatically establish**:

> God's precise reason for choosing that form.

The correct sequence is:

> **observation → demonstrable effect → cautious inference about purpose**

unless purpose itself is disclosed.

---

# 9. Translation Audit

For claims materially affected by translation, classify them as:

### Translation-invariant

The judgment survives the meaningful translations considered.

### Translation-resilient

Wording/nuance changes, but the core judgment survives.

### Translation-sensitive

Translation materially affects what can responsibly be claimed.

Do not use these labels decoratively.

They tell the reader what the judgment has survived.

---

# 10. Ambiguity Does Not Mean Emptiness

When translation or interpretation remains unresolved, ask:

> **What survives the disagreement?**

For example, disagreement about precise identification may leave stable:

* placement;
* movement;
* sequence;
* grammatical relation;
* narrative context.

The audit should preserve what remains knowable rather than forcing:

> certainty

or:

> agnosticism.

---

# 11. Negative Findings Are Genuine Findings

A Claim Audit must be able to record:

> **The evidence does not presently authorize X.**

This is not weaker scholarship.

Examples include:

* a word does not itself establish moral evil;
* a scene does not narrate divine panic;
* a later metaphor should not automatically be imported backward.

Negative findings often provide the most important protection against overclaiming.

---

# 12. External Thinkers Are Dialogue Partners, Not Authorities to Stack

When scholarship or other thinkers enter the investigation, ask of each:

> **What does this thinker help us see?**

and:

> **What does this thinker prevent us from claiming?**

Do not research only for agreement.

Where serious counterevidence exists, preserve it.

The strongest judgment may emerge because disagreement forced the claim to become narrower.

---

# 13. Do Not Manufacture False Balance

Preserving competing interpretations does not require treating them as equally strong.

Record whether an alternative is:

* strong;
* plausible;
* possible but weaker;
* substantially challenged;
* presently rejected;
* unresolved.

Proportionality applies to alternatives too.

---

# 14. Human Experience Is Correspondence, Not Authorization

Analogies and lived experience may:

* illuminate;
* test relational plausibility;
* help readers understand an inference;
* reveal practical implications.

They must not manufacture the exegesis.

The order is:

> **text/evidence → judgment → human correspondence**

not:

> **emotionally useful idea → therefore textual meaning.**

---

# 15. Canonical Sequencing

When later Scripture is relevant:

> **Let the beginning remain a beginning.**

First determine what the local passage gives.

Then allow later revelation to:

* corroborate;
* test;
* deepen;
* complicate;
* correct interpretation.

Do not import later doctrine backward and then present it as though the earlier passage had explicitly supplied it.

Likewise, do not use an earlier passage to prevent later revelation from saying something genuinely new.

---

# 16. Corrigible Recognition

Distinguish:

> **revelation**

from:

> **our recognition of revelation.**

Later evidence may correct our interpretation without requiring the conclusion that the earlier revelation itself was defective.

The working posture is:

> **Carry what has been responsibly seen without making it more than it is or less than it is.**

---

# 17. Recognizable Does Not Mean Predictable

Do not allow a first picture of God to become the authority over later evidence.

A genuine recognition may be:

* deepened;
* complicated;
* corrected;
* surprised.

Therefore:

> **Do not defend the portrait. Continue knowing the Person.**

---

# 18. Identify Material Research Gaps

After provisional adjudication, ask:

> **Is additional research actually necessary to assign a responsible present status?**

If no:

> do not research merely because more scholarship exists.

If yes:

> identify the smallest question preventing adjudication.

---

# 19. Smallest Necessary Research Pass

Research only enough to resolve the identified gap.

Do not convert retrospective auditing into a completely new investigation.

The Genesis 1:1–2 process demonstrated this successfully when the relational-correction claim required only a bounded canonical pass involving a small set of disciple narratives.

---

# 20. Argument Records Come Before the Public Table

Create the durable reasoning record before finalizing the public summary.

An Argument Record should normally contain:

* Claim
* Present status
* Primary evidence
* Why we presently hold it
* Translation/source-language evidence
* External dialogue
* Counterevidence / complication
* Competing interpretations
* Canonical correspondence
* Human-experience correspondence
* Present judgment
* Previous formulation / revision
* What could change this status?
* Sources

Not every section is mandatory when irrelevant.

But counterevidence and status reasoning should not disappear.

---

# 21. Record Types

The Genesis audit system now recognizes four durable provenance objects.

## AR — Argument Record

Answers:

> **Why do we presently hold this judgment?**

ARs contain evidence, reasoning, counterevidence, alternatives, confidence, and change conditions.

---

## RR — Revision Record

Answers:

> **How did an existing judgment change?**

Use when:

* wording weakened;
* confidence changed;
* an interpretation was corrected;
* a distinction materially changed the judgment.

Standard structure:

> Earlier formulation
> What challenged it
> Present formulation

Do not use RR for ordinary copyediting.

---

## ER — Emergent Record

Answers:

> **What genuinely new insight emerged after the original investigation/publication?**

ER is **not** a Revision Record.

Nothing necessarily had to be wrong before.

ER preserves:

* date/origin;
* later observation;
* new research;
* counterargument;
* adjudication;
* publication effect.

This prevents later discoveries from being retroactively inserted into the original intellectual history.

---

## OQ — Open Question

Answers:

> **What question is worth preserving even though the evidence does not presently authorize resolution?**

An OQ prevents:

* forgetting the question;
* forcing premature closure;
* quietly treating a hypothesis as a finding.

---

# 22. Stable Provenance IDs

Once created, IDs should remain stable.

Do not renumber an AR because:

* another AR is added;
* a public row merges multiple ARs;
* an ER appears later.

The record type itself carries historical meaning.

---

# 23. Select the Public Claims Only After Adjudication

The public Claim Audit should normally contain approximately:

> **8–12 consequential claims**

not every observation.

Select claims that:

* carry substantial argumentative weight;
* would materially affect interpretation if wrong;
* expose important confidence differences;
* reveal consequential uncertainty or correction.

---

# 24. Public Claim Audit Structure

Each public row should expose at minimum:

| Claim | Present Status | Why This Status? | Evidence & Dialogue |
| ----- | -------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- |

The final column should link to the AR(s).

Where an ER later expands a public claim, distinguish:

> **Original Argument Record**

from:

> **Later Development.**

---

# 25. Mandatory Status Disclaimer

Every public Claim Audit should state:

> **Present status means present status. New evidence may strengthen, weaken, revise, or overturn any judgment above.**

This is not boilerplate.

It communicates the epistemic posture of the entire project.

---

# 26. Build the Revision Record Before Reconciling Published Prose

Once the ARs and public claims are stable, construct the material Revision Record.

Only then compare the published essay against the adjudicated judgments.

This prevents publication status from controlling the audit.

---

# 27. Published-Prose Reconciliation

For each consequential published formulation classify:

### Appropriately stated

Matches the adjudicated judgment.

### Appropriately simplified

Less technical than the AR but suitable for an accessible essay.

### Stronger than authorized

Exceeds the evidence/status.

### Weaker than authorized

Unnecessarily understates a strong finding.

### Missing qualification

Core claim survives but requires a visible boundary.

### Potentially misleading without AR

Accessible wording may remain because the linked audit provides necessary precision.

---

# 28. Do Not Make the Essay Sound Like the Audit

This is essential.

The essay and audit perform different functions.

> **Essay = encounter.**

> **Audit = inspection.**

Memorable language may remain in the essay even when its AR requires more technical classification.

The audit exists partly to preserve accessibility without sacrificing rigor.

---

# 29. Protect Praxis

Every movement should eventually bring the reader face to face with:

> **What might this responsibly show me about God?**

and:

> **What could that mean for my relationship with Him?**

But praxis must remain downstream from evidence.

Relational usefulness cannot authorize interpretation.

---

# 30. Post-Publication Discovery Gate

When a new insight emerges after publication:

Do **not** simply insert it.

Run:

> observation
> → preliminary classification
> → external dialogue if needed
> → counterargument
> → adjudication
> → provenance classification
> → publication decision.

If genuinely new:

> **ER**

not RR.

---

# 31. Publication Decision for an ER

An adjudicated ER may:

* remain research-only;
* expand an existing AR;
* become a later-development link under an existing public claim;
* authorize a small essay addition;
* require a new public claim;
* generate a future essay instead.

Choose the smallest publication effect proportionate to its significance.

---

# 32. Do Not Let a Large Insight Hijack a Small Essay

A discovery may have enormous implications without those implications belonging in the present essay.

Use:

> **Signal the magnitude. Do not exhaust the implications.**

Park larger questions as OQs or future-work records.

---

# 33. Codex Packaging

Once intellectual adjudication is complete, Codex should normally receive **two authoritative artifacts**:

### Implementation Handoff

Tells Codex:

> **what to do.**

### Audit Dossier

Tells Codex:

> **what the adjudicated judgments actually are.**

Do not make Codex reconstruct settled ARs from abbreviated implementation instructions.

---

# 34. Codex Canonical-Source Gate

Codex must inspect the current canonical source before changing prose.

Historical revision records do not prove an old formulation still survives.

Only modify prose when the current canonical wording actually conflicts with the adjudicated audit or when a specifically authorized ER requires an addition.

---

# 35. Protected Language

Before implementation, identify memorable essay formulations that should not be flattened merely because AR language is more technical.

Codex should receive a **protected formulations list**.

---

# 36. Required Implementation Classification

For each potential prose change, Codex should report:

> **REQUIRED — CHANGED**

> **RECOMMENDED — CHANGED WITH JUSTIFICATION**

> **RECOMMENDED — LEFT UNCHANGED**

> **NO CHANGE — PROTECTED**

For new ER material:

> **POST-PUBLICATION DEVELOPMENT — ADDED**

---

# 37. Source Integrity

Before publication:

* verify author;
* verify work;
* verify bibliographic metadata;
* verify quotation;
* verify page number if used;
* verify that the source actually supports the attributed claim.

Do not fabricate missing bibliographic detail.

If a new source is added after the original investigation, distinguish:

> **original evidentiary source**

from:

> **post-investigation verification/support.**

---

# 38. Implementation Validation

At minimum verify:

* canonical source;
* Claim Audit route;
* public claim count;
* AR links;
* RR links;
* ER/OQ labeling;
* status disclaimer;
* source links;
* build;
* sitemap/navigation where applicable;
* responsive/mobile behavior where tooling permits.

---

# 39. Implementation Gate Review

After Codex returns its report, review:

### Intellectual fidelity

Did statuses survive?

### Provenance fidelity

Are AR/RR/ER/OQ distinctions preserved?

### Rhetorical fidelity

Did the essay remain an essay?

### Relational fidelity

Did praxis remain connected to evidence?

### Technical fidelity

Do routes, links, sources, and builds work?

Only then close the essay.

---

# 40. Do Not Hide Correction

A revision is not evidence that the process failed.

If new evidence changed the judgment, the process worked.

The publication architecture should make that inspectable.

Likewise:

> a later discovery is not evidence that the original essay was defective.

That is why ER exists.

---

# 41. Governing Confidence Posture

The audit should neither inflate nor diminish.

Do not make an inference sound like a textual given.

Do not make a high-confidence finding sound tentative merely because scholarship exists.

Do not make ambiguity global.

Do not make confidence immune to revision.

The target is:

> **proportionate confidence.**

---

# 42. Governing Relational Posture

The project is ultimately concerned with knowing God through biblical revelation.

Therefore the method should allow a reader to say:

> **I think I have responsibly seen something true here.**

without requiring:

> **I have exhausted what can be known.**

And:

> **I remain open to correction.**

without requiring:

> **I know nothing.**

That posture connects epistemic discipline with relationship.

---

# 44. The Cycle Is Open, but the Current Judgment Is Real

The statement:

> **the cycle remains open to new evidence**

must not be misunderstood as:

> no judgment is ever stable enough to use.

A present judgment can be:

* well supported;
* high confidence;
* appropriate for teaching;
* appropriate for publication;
* appropriate for relational response;

while still remaining answerable to future evidence.

Corrigibility is not indecision.

The governing posture is:

> **Hold the judgment as strongly as the evidence presently authorizes—and no more weakly than the evidence presently authorizes.**

---

# 45. Distinguish Humility From Hesitation

The methodology should not reward constant qualification for its own sake.

Humility means:

> **I will not claim more than I have evidence to claim.**

It also means:

> **I will not pretend I know less than the evidence permits me to know.**

Therefore:

> **high-confidence finding**

is entirely compatible with epistemic humility.

So is:

> **not presently authorized.**

Both are proportional judgments.

---

# 46. Distinguish Mystery From Uncertainty

These should not be treated as synonyms.

### Uncertainty

Concerns our confidence about a particular judgment.

### Mystery

Concerns reality exceeding what has presently been disclosed or comprehended.

A reader may be highly confident about something while recognizing enormous mystery beyond it.

For example:

> **God is presented as Creator**

may be held strongly while:

> **why creation exists at all**

remains profoundly open.

The existence of mystery does not automatically lower confidence in what has actually been disclosed.

---

# 47. Distinguish Silence From Negation

If a passage does not say X, the audit may conclude:

> **this passage does not presently authorize X.**

It may not automatically conclude:

> **X is false.**

This distinction is especially important in a sequential reading method.

Later revelation may eventually:

* affirm X;
* reject X;
* complicate X;
* leave X unresolved.

Therefore:

> **not given here**

does not automatically mean:

> **never true.**

This is another expression of:

> **Not first does not mean not ever.**

---

# 48. Distinguish Negative Findings From Arguments From Silence

A negative finding is strongest when the claim is:

> **The text does not identify X as Y.**

It becomes much weaker when transformed into:

> **Because the text does not identify X as Y, X cannot possibly be Y.**

The first may be direct observation.

The second requires additional reasoning.

Every retrospective audit should inspect this boundary carefully.

---

# 49. The Method Must Be Able to Correct Itself

The protocol itself is not exempt from WhyDive.

If later audits reveal that:

* a status category is inadequate;
* an AR field is unnecessary;
* ER and RR need further differentiation;
* canonical sequencing produces an unforeseen problem;
* reader-effect analysis is being overused;
* public audits are too dense;

revise the protocol.

Record meaningful protocol changes by version.

Do not protect GRA-01 merely because it has become governance.

# 52. What These Implementations Taught Us

Several methodological discoveries should now be considered durable unless later evidence challenges them.

### First

> **The strongest claim may be narrower than the most compelling claim.**

Narrowing often increased confidence.

### Second

> **Uncertainty can be local.**

A disputed word does not make an entire passage unknowable.

### Third

> **Negative findings matter.**

“What the evidence does not authorize” can materially change one's picture of God.

### Fourth

> **Effect and purpose require separation.**

This prevented repeated overclaims about divine psychology and pedagogy.

### Fifth

> **Later revelation needs genuine corrective authority.**

Otherwise sequential reading becomes confirmation bias.

### Sixth

> **Correction and later discovery require different provenance objects.**

Hence RR and ER.

### Seventh

> **The essay and audit need different rhetorical jobs.**

Accessibility and rigor do not require the same artifact.

# 53. Required Retrospective Audit Workflow

The governed sequence for future essays is now:

### Phase 1 — Establish scope

Identify:

* controlling passage;
* published essay;
* investigation history;
* current canonical source.

### Phase 2 — Reconstruct

Recover the actual investigation.

### Phase 3 — Inventory

Build the Master Claim Inventory.

### Phase 4 — Cluster

Group claims conceptually.

### Phase 5 — Provisional adjudication

Create provisional ARs and statuses.

### Phase 6 — Methodological audits

Run:

* effect/purpose;
* translation;
* local-confidence;
* canonical-sequencing;
* human-correspondence;
* negative-finding audits.

### Phase 7 — Gap detection

Identify only material evidentiary gaps.

### Phase 8 — Bounded research

Run the smallest necessary research pass.

### Phase 9 — Final ARs

Finalize durable Argument Records.

### Phase 10 — Revision history

Build the RR layer.

### Phase 11 — Public selection

Choose approximately 8–12 consequential claims.

### Phase 12 — Public Claim Audit

Generate present-status table.

### Phase 13 — Published-prose reconciliation

Audit the actual canonical essay.

### Phase 14 — Later-development check

Classify later insights as ER or OQ where appropriate.

### Phase 15 — Codex package

Produce:

* Implementation Handoff;
* Authoritative Audit Dossier.

### Phase 16 — Implementation

Codex modifies canonical artifacts.

### Phase 17 — Gate review

Verify intellectual, provenance, rhetorical, and technical fidelity.

### Phase 18 — Close

Declare current audit state complete while leaving judgments corrigible.

This order should not be casually rearranged.

# 54. Minimum Audit Deliverables

A completed retrospective audit should normally produce:

1. **Master Claim Inventory**
2. **Final Argument Records**
3. **Revision Record**
4. **8–12-row Public Claim Audit**
5. **Open Questions**, where applicable
6. **Emergent Records**, where applicable
7. **Published-Prose Reconciliation**
8. **Protected Formulations List**
9. **Codex Implementation Handoff**
10. **Authoritative Audit Dossier**
11. **Implementation Gate Review**

Not every essay will require ER or OQ records.

Do not create empty record types merely for consistency.

---

# 55. Minimum AR Quality Gate

An Argument Record is not complete merely because it contains citations.

Before finalization ask:

> What exactly is the claim?

> What kind of claim is it?

> What evidence directly supports it?

> What evidence complicates it?

> What alternatives remain?

> What is inference rather than textual given?

> What translation issues matter?

> Did later Scripture contribute?

> Did human experience merely illustrate?

> What is our present status?

> What would change that status?

If those questions cannot be answered, the AR is not ready for publication.

---

# 56. Minimum Public Claim Quality Gate

A public claim should not be selected merely because it is memorable.

Ask:

> Would changing this judgment materially change the essay?

If no, it may not deserve a public row.

Also ask:

> Does this row expose something important about confidence, uncertainty, or provenance?

The public audit should reveal the **architecture of judgment**, not summarize the essay.

---

# 57. Minimum RR Quality Gate

A Revision Record requires a **material judgment change**.

Good RR:

> “God is calm” → narration does not depict panic.

Not RR:

> “God acts.” → “God acts decisively.”

unless the change represents an evidentiary shift.

Revision history should remain intellectually meaningful.

---

# 58. Minimum ER Quality Gate

A later idea becomes an Emergent Record only when:

1. it genuinely arose after the original investigation/publication;
2. it is consequential;
3. it has been subjected to the appropriate evidence test;
4. counterarguments have been considered;
5. present status has been assigned.

A new thought is not automatically an ER.

It begins as:

> **candidate emergent insight.**

Only adjudication promotes it.

---

# 59. Minimum OQ Quality Gate

An Open Question should be:

* consequential;
* genuinely unresolved;
* connected to the investigation;
* potentially researchable.

Do not create OQs for every curiosity.

An OQ preserves a boundary worth remembering.

---

# 60. Final Essay Gate

Before declaring an essay audit complete, ask:

### Textual fidelity

Have we allowed the passage to say what it says—and stopped where it stops?

### Evidentiary fidelity

Does every confidence level match its evidence?

### Translation fidelity

Have language differences been localized properly?

### Scholarly fidelity

Have serious countervoices been represented proportionally?

### Canonical fidelity

Did later revelation arrive later?

### Relational fidelity

Did praxis arise from the evidence rather than authorize it?

### Provenance fidelity

Can the reader distinguish original judgment, revision, later discovery, and open question?

### Rhetorical fidelity

Did the essay remain accessible rather than becoming an academic dossier?

If any answer is materially no, the audit remains open.

---

# 61. Final Project Principle

The deepest purpose of this architecture is not to create more documentation.

It is to make **responsible knowing visible**.

The reader should be able to see:

> what we noticed;

> what we inferred;

> where language complicated the inference;

> what other thinkers challenged;

> what survived;

> what weakened;

> what changed;

> what remains unresolved;

> what we discovered later;

> and why we presently hold the judgment we do.

That visibility is itself part of the theological posture.

---

# 62. Final Relational Principle

The Character-of-God project does not ask the reader to choose between:

> confidence

and:

> humility.

Nor between:

> relationship

and:

> correction.

Nor between:

> genuine knowledge

and:

> mystery.

The methodology instead seeks a posture in which a reader can say:

> **I will receive what has been given.**

> **I will not make it more than it is.**

> **I will not make it less than it is.**

> **I will remain answerable to what comes next.**

> **And I will continue knowing the Person rather than defending my portrait of Him.**
