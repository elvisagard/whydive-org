# WhyDive.org Midjourney Image Prompt Plan

## Purpose

These prompts support the first visual system for WhyDive.org.

The images should make the site feel contemplative, authoritative, and movement-oriented without making it feel like a product site, school site, SaaS site, or generic AI brand.

Use images sparingly. The writing should remain the center of gravity.

## Global Visual Rules

Preferred qualities:

- Quiet depth
- Light through darkness
- Evidence becoming visible
- Reflection
- Thresholds
- Scholarly atmosphere
- Human scale
- Editorial restraint
- Warm institutional seriousness

Avoid:

- Classrooms
- Corporate teams
- Product mockups
- Literal robots or glowing brains
- Blue-purple SaaS gradients
- Decorative orbs
- Stock-photo smiles
- Overly mystical fantasy symbolism
- Crowded detail

Suggested Midjourney style terms:

- contemplative editorial photography
- cinematic natural light
- museum-grade composition
- muted warm neutrals
- deep ink shadows
- restrained teal and old-gold accents
- philosophical institute
- quiet archival atmosphere
- negative space for text

## Prompt Family 9: Spaces Documentary Images

Use:

Spaces index, individual Space pages, relatable essays, and case-study style pages.

Goal:

Show the utility of the framework inside real human settings. These images should draw readers into scenarios where evidence, interpretation, judgment, and action matter. They should feel closer to documentary journalism than symbolic institute imagery.

This is the closest visual analog to the Chart-Ed relatable-page pattern, but translated for WhyDive. The images should make visitors feel: "this is about people like me facing consequential claims."

Documentary rules:

- Use natural or available light.
- Show real working environments, not polished marketing scenes.
- Prefer candid attention, discussion, reading, annotation, and listening.
- Use authentic clothing, tools, and room details.
- Include evidence objects where possible: printed reports, notebooks, charts, maps, primary sources, meeting notes, tablets, dashboards, field notes.
- Keep screens, documents, and signs unreadable.
- Avoid logos, branded products, staged smiles, obvious stock-photo composition, exaggerated emotions, and visual effects.
- Avoid making the scene feel like a corporate DEI brochure, a classroom brochure, a tech startup ad, or spiritual wellness branding.

## Notes for Selection

Choose images that:

- Leave space for text
- Feel quiet before they feel impressive
- Support long-form reading
- Suggest inquiry rather than certainty
- Preserve seriousness without becoming cold

Reject images that:

- Look like a school website
- Look like an AI startup
- Look like generic leadership coaching
- Look like spiritual wellness branding
- Distract from the writing
